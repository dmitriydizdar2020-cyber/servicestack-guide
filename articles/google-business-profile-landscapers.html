Google Business Profile for landscapers: service areas without fake locations

A practical guide to service-area settings, honest address display, photos and verification for contractors who travel to customers.

SERVICE STACK EDITORIAL DESK · NOV 13, 2026 · DOCUMENTATION-BASED GUIDE

Editorial transparency: This guide is based on linked official documentation and an original editorial decision framework, not hands-on testing or measured customer results. No paid referral links are included. Product features, prices and policies may change. All images are synthetic editorial illustrations, never real client work. Our editorial standards.

The short answer: If your landscaping company visits customers and does not receive them at your address, Google says to hide the address and use an accurate service area. A virtual office is not a substitute for an eligible business location.

In this guide:

  1. Identify whether you are a service-area or hybrid business
  2. Set realistic service areas
  3. Verify the business before assuming visibility
  4. Show real work and explain the process
  5. Connect the profile to a useful website

Identify whether you are a service-area or hybrid business

Google distinguishes businesses that visit customers without receiving them at a business address from hybrid businesses that also serve people at a staffed storefront. Many landscaping companies fit the service-area model. If customers do not visit your address, Google's guidance says to remove that address from the public Business Profile.

Do not display a home address as a showroom or rent an unstaffed virtual office merely to create a map pin. A genuine showroom with permanent signage and staff may be different; check the current eligibility rules before editing your profile.

Set realistic service areas

Google's current help documentation allows up to 20 service areas specified as cities, postal codes or other supported areas; it does not let you define a simple radius. It recommends that the overall boundary generally be within about two hours' driving distance of the base.

List places you actually serve, not every wealthy suburb in the region. If you have different crews and separately eligible staffed locations, evaluate each location against Google's rules rather than creating duplicate profiles for the same business.

Verify the business before assuming visibility

Google's documentation notes that Business Profile photos and videos appear only after profile verification. Verification methods and timing can vary. Complete the required verification process through the official interface; do not pay someone promising guaranteed approval.

Make sure the business name, category, phone, website and hours match reality. Use a real phone that is answered and an email address the owner controls. Keep recovery access secure and document who administers the profile.

Show real work and explain the process

Google recommends clear, well-lit photographs with minimal alterations. For a landscaper, choose permissioned job photos that accurately represent services and progress. The photo guidance describes JPG or PNG formats and recommended dimensions; these are not a guarantee that an image will be selected as the cover.

Avoid AI-generated “completed projects” presented as actual jobs. If you lack permission to publish a private garden, use authentic equipment or team photos, or explain your work without misleading visual evidence. Do not fabricate reviews or incentives for positive ratings.

Connect the profile to a useful website

Link to a site that explains real services, the actual service area, examples and a working quote path. A website and a Business Profile serve complementary jobs; neither guarantees top rankings. Google says LocalBusiness structured data can help it understand relevant details, but it does not guarantee a rich result.

Monitor actual calls and enquiry quality where privacy and consent allow. If a visitor clicks from Maps and then finds a broken form or unrelated landing page, fixing that journey is more valuable than adding twenty near-identical town pages.

A practical pre-launch decision checklist

Use these questions with your actual business workflow, not with a demo or a hypothetical sales forecast. A “yes” should mean you have verified it with your own site, customer journey or authorized business records.

  • Is the public address hidden when customers cannot visit?
  • Are service areas real and within practical travel range?
  • Are photos permissioned and unaltered?
  • Has the profile actually been verified?

If any answer is unknown, assign a real person to test it before spending money or promising customers a result. For a related starting point, see our landscaping homepage guide and quote-request form guide.
