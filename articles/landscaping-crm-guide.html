CRM for a Landscaping Business: A Practical Buyer’s Guide

How to move from scattered enquiries to an accountable sales pipeline. Compare spreadsheets, HubSpot and Pipedrive, then build your own workflow and first-year budget.

The short answerStart with the process, not the software. If one person handles a handful of quotes each week, a well-maintained spreadsheet may be enough. If enquiries regularly fall between the cracks, you need a clearly owned sales pipeline and reminders. HubSpot offers a free starting point; Pipedrive focuses on moving deals through visible stages. Pick the least complicated option your team will actually keep up to date.

Why a landscaping team loses track of legitimate enquiries

A homeowner asks about a new patio. Someone answers the phone, writes the details on paper, and promises to follow up. The estimator visits on Friday. Two weeks later nobody can remember whether the quote was sent—or who was supposed to call. This is a workflow problem, not automatically a software problem.

A useful CRM records who owns the next action, what the customer asked for and when someone will respond. The software should make those three details easier to see. It should not become another daily administrative job for an already busy crew.

The seven stages worth tracking

Don't begin with a list of forty database fields. Map the actual progression from enquiry to booked job, keeping delivery work separate from the sales decision.

StageWhat it meansOne decision the owner must make
01 · New enquiryPhone, website or referral request has arrived.Who responds, and is the service area a match?
02 · QualifiedService, location and basic scope fit.Does this require a call, photos or an on-site visit?
03 · Site visit scheduledA real appointment has been agreed.Who attends and which site details are needed?
04 · Estimate in progressTeam is preparing its scope and price.Who approves the quote and when?
05 · Proposal sentA specific estimate has reached the prospect.Is there a clear next check-in date?
06 · Decision pendingProspect has not yet accepted or declined.What genuine question can we help answer?
07 · Won / lostJob awarded, declined or no longer viable.Was the outcome recorded, and can we learn why?

These stages are an original example process, not the default settings in any particular vendor. Simplify them if your company only needs four steps. For repeat lawn maintenance, a shorter route from lead to scheduled start date may work better than the full design-project sequence.

Five questions to ask before paying

  1. Can a new form response become a lead without retyping everything, and is that integration included in the tier you choose?
  2. Can a second team member see exactly which estimates need action today?
  3. Are there limits on users, contacts, active deals, automation or stored documents?
  4. If you stop paying, how will you export contacts and opportunity history?
  5. Who will own data retention, permissions and any customer consent?

A one-week CRM setup plan

  1. Day 1 — Audit the last ten real enquiries. Where were they recorded? Which ones had no next owner or scheduled response? Avoid copying sensitive customer details into a public document.
  2. Day 2 — Choose four to seven useful stages. Name each stage after a real action rather than vague labels such as “working.”
  3. Day 3 — Pick an accountable owner. A lead without a next owner is not ready to move forward.
  4. Day 4 — Enter only necessary data. Service requested, approximate location, customer contact where authorized, proposed value, next action and due date are often enough.
  5. Day 5 — Test a real handoff. Use a test customer record, run a dummy form and confirm the assigned teammate sees the follow-up.
  6. Day 6 — Review overdue actions. Can you identify every quote awaiting an update in under two minutes?
  7. Day 7 — Decide whether the tool helped. If work is slower than your original spreadsheet, simplify before paying for a larger plan.

Keep contact access restricted to the people who need it. Apply your actual privacy notice and applicable data-protection rules; a CRM is not permission to send unsolicited promotional messages. For follow-up wording, use our estimate follow-up templates.
