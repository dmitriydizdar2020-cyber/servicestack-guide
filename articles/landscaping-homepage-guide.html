WEBSITE STRATEGY / ORIGINAL WORKSHEET

A landscaping homepage that makes it easier to request an estimate.

The job of your homepage is not to impress another website designer. It is to help the right homeowner decide whether you do their kind of project, work in their area and offer a sensible way to get in touch.

SERVICE STACK EDITORIAL DESK · FIRST PUBLISHED NOV 12, 2026 · RESEARCH-BASED WORKING GUIDE

Editorial transparency: This is an original planning framework informed by public Google guidance, not measured conversion research or claimed testing of real contractor websites. It has no paid referral links. The cover image is an AI-generated editorial illustration of a fictional worksite.

The short answer: Put your actual service, genuine service area, credible evidence and one easy next step above the fold. Show specific services and real authorized projects below. Make the request workflow work on a phone before spending money on ads.

Imagine two visitors. One wants weekly lawn maintenance; the other needs a drainage problem assessed. They need different answers and may require different estimates. A homepage can direct both to the right next step without trying to explain every service in the first screen.

In this guide: first-screen example · seven page sections · real evidence · service areas · free homepage brief · pre-launch checks.

1. Write the first screen for a homeowner, not a marketing award

A useful first screen needs four answers: what you do, where you work, why the visitor should investigate further, and how to request the next step. Use the language actual customers use. Avoid unsupported claims such as “#1 in the city” or “guaranteed lowest price.”

EXAMPLE COPY / FICTIONAL CONTRACTOR

Patios, drainage and landscape installation in [your actual towns]

See the kinds of projects our crew handles, where we work and how our estimate process starts. Share a few details about your project and we’ll explain the next step.

Primary action: Request an estimate   Secondary: Call the team

Only promise a response time, license or warranty when the business can substantiate it. Replace placeholders with verified information.

When choosing between a quote form and a booking calendar, decide whether every enquiry needs a site visit or a preliminary conversation. A request for an estimate is not a confirmed appointment. See our quote-request form specification for a practical intake workflow.

2. The seven sections worth building before adding more pages

  1. Hero: a specific service promise, service area and clear next step. Use a real job photo when you have permission; otherwise a neutral non-deceptive visual.
  2. Services: three to six real categories such as patios, retaining walls, drainage and recurring maintenance. Explain who each is for; don't list services you do not provide.
  3. Project evidence: a few authentic finished jobs with clear captions identifying work performed and permission to publish. Do not use AI pictures as customer portfolio evidence.
  4. Service area: towns or postal regions you genuinely serve. Link to unique local information only when you have real location-specific content.
  5. Process: how a request is reviewed, whether a visit is needed, when the customer may expect the next update, and what a quote includes.
  6. Questions: answer practical doubts: minimum project size, maintenance vs installation, quotation process and seasonal availability. Don't invent fixed prices.
  7. Final contact: a short form plus a working tap-to-call link, with a privacy explanation and an accessible confirmation.

Resist the temptation to put every service, awards badge and pop-up above the fold. If each new block doesn't answer a real buyer question, it probably belongs elsewhere or nowhere.

3. Proof is more persuasive when the details are real

Use permissioned project photography with an accurate description: “Patio replacement, approximate size, material and neighborhood” only if the details are confirmed and the client permits publication. A before/after pair is informative when the images are genuinely of the same project. A synthetic image may decorate a page, but must not impersonate a finished job.

Only publish testimonials you are authorized to share, with a true representation of the customer's experience. Avoid invented names, fake review scores and unverifiable “featured in” logos. If the business has no portfolio yet, a clear explanation of its process, actual qualifications and transparent scope is more trustworthy than fabricated proof.

4. Match geographic claims to the real business

Be precise about where you serve and don't create dozens of nearly identical city pages. If you use Google Business Profile, its current guidance distinguishes service-area businesses from storefronts and asks businesses to represent actual service areas accurately. These Business Profile rules are not a guarantee that adding town names to a website will improve rankings.

Google's guidance for service-area businesses says that businesses visiting customers should set their service areas accurately. Businesses that do not serve customers at their address should not present it as a customer-facing storefront. Check the primary Google Business Profile service-area documentation and confirm what applies to your model.

5. Build the enquiry path before buying additional tools

Ask only what the team needs at first contact: service type, broad location, a short description and a chosen reply method. If photos are optional, explain acceptable uploads and retention. Test the form on a phone and confirm the email or CRM destination receives a real test submission. Keep a manual fallback: a visible phone link can rescue a failed form.

Assign each enquiry to a person or named inbox and keep a simple status: new, contacted, visit scheduled, estimate sent, won or closed. Our CRM buyer guide explains when a spreadsheet is enough and when a CRM may help. Our follow-up templates help you write respectful updates after quoting.

7. Launch checks that matter more than flashy effects

  • At 390px mobile width, can you read the headline and use the primary action without sideways scrolling?
  • Does the tap-to-call number work? Does the quote form really reach the right inbox?
  • Can users use the form with a keyboard and screen reader labels? Are photos described meaningfully?
  • Are all service areas, warranties, credentials and project images accurate and permissioned?
  • Does each service button lead to the right section instead of a generic home page?
  • Do title, meta description, canonical URL and content match the page's actual purpose?
  • Is your contact-data collection appropriate for the customer's location and explained clearly?
