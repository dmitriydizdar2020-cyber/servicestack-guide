SITE123 launch checklist for a local service business

A practical preflight sequence for a contractor site: real services, honest photos, contact delivery, indexing, ownership and the plan features worth verifying.

SERVICE STACK EDITORIAL DESK · NOV 13, 2026 · DOCUMENTATION-BASED GUIDE

Editorial transparency: This guide is based on linked official documentation and an original editorial decision framework, not hands-on testing or measured customer results. No paid referral links are included. Product features, prices and policies may change. All images are synthetic editorial illustrations, never real client work. Our editorial standards.

The short answer: Before buying a plan or promoting a site, confirm that a homeowner can understand your work, submit a real enquiry, and find accurate contact information on a phone. Feature availability is plan-dependent.

In this guide:

  1. Build the minimum useful site first
  2. Check the domain and plan before paying
  3. Make every enquiry route observable
  4. Prepare search discovery without promising rankings
  5. Run a phone-first launch rehearsal

Build the minimum useful site first

For a one-person landscaping business, start with a homepage, three or four genuine service descriptions, a small authorized project gallery, a service-area explanation and a contact route. This is not a requirement to purchase a template or add a blog.

Use plain headlines such as “Patio installation and drainage in [actual area].” Replace every placeholder before launch. Never leave demo testimonials, generic certifications or a map pin at an address where customers cannot visit.

Check the domain and plan before paying

SITE123 offers website creation and documents domain management options, but domain connection, storage, form capabilities and other entitlements should be verified against the plan offered to your account today. Its support documentation explains custom-domain management; it is not a promise that every plan includes a custom domain.

Write down what you need before opening checkout: custom domain, working forms, no platform branding if relevant, analytics options, redirects and export or migration possibilities. Compare the full renewal price, not only a promotional first period.

Make every enquiry route observable

Create a test enquiry using a non-sensitive test identity. Confirm the message reaches the correct mailbox, that a confirmation is shown, and that the reply address works. SITE123's form support describes customizable fields and auto-replies; actual behavior must be tested in the chosen configuration.

Use one primary call to action and a visible tap-to-call fallback. Do not collect sensitive customer details you do not need. Add a short privacy notice explaining what happens to the information and who receives it.

Prepare search discovery without promising rankings

Give each useful page a distinct descriptive title, a readable heading and internal links. Set a canonical URL, ensure the page is not marked noindex when approved for release, and include genuinely public pages in a sitemap. Google explains that structured data and sitemaps can help it understand or discover pages, not guarantee display.

If the platform exposes an indexing toggle, inspect its output rather than trusting the label. Test the public URL without logging in. Search Console URL Inspection is the correct place to check Google's actual view; do not treat a live page as automatically indexed.

Run a phone-first launch rehearsal

At a 390px viewport, check the first screen, navigation, gallery and form labels. Repeat on tablet and desktop. Ask someone unfamiliar with the site to locate the service area, see an example of real work and request a quote.

Before announcing the site, record the domain owner, renewal date, admin access recovery method and a backup of your copy and images. A contractor should not lose customer enquiries because the only login belongs to a freelance designer.

A practical pre-launch decision checklist

Use these questions with your actual business workflow, not with a demo or a hypothetical sales forecast. A “yes” should mean you have verified it with your own site, customer journey or authorized business records.

  • Did a test form arrive at the correct inbox?
  • Are domain, renewal and export restrictions documented?
  • Are project images and reviews authentic?
  • Are public pages indexable only after QA?

If any answer is unknown, assign a real person to test it before spending money or promising customers a result. For a related starting point, see our landscaping homepage guide and quote-request form guide.
