A landscaping company needs a credible place to show its work, but it also needs a way to turn enquiries into conversations. These two platforms solve overlapping—and importantly different—parts of that problem.

Two different jobs, one paying customer

Imagine a homeowner searching for “patio contractor near me.” They want to know whether you work in their area, whether your past projects look credible, which services you offer, and how to ask for a quote. That journey needs a discoverable business website. It might include a homepage, pages for drainage and hardscaping, a gallery, testimonials that actually belong to your business, an About page, and a contact form.

Now imagine the same contractor sending a targeted email to former enquiries about spring patio consultations. The reader should see one clear offer, one next step and a relevant follow-up. That journey might benefit from a dedicated funnel page and email sequence. It does not replace the trust signals a real business website needs.

This distinction matters because buying software on the basis of impressive demos can leave you with a landing page that does not answer a homeowner's basic questions—or a beautiful brochure site whose enquiries disappear into an inbox nobody checks.

A real-world workflow to design—not a fabricated case study

Consider a fictional example of a small patio contractor serving two nearby towns. The operator wants to know not just where a lead came from, but what needs to happen before anyone books the work. Here is the process we would specify before choosing technology:

  1. Discovery: the homeowner searches for patio installation or repairs and lands on a specific service page.
  2. Confidence: the page shows actual photos of relevant work, a clear service area and evidence-based details about the business—no invented reviews.
  3. Enquiry: the form asks for the type of job, approximate location, preferred reply method and optional useful information. Don't ask for unnecessary personal data.
  4. Acknowledgement: a clearly stated confirmation tells the visitor when they can expect a response. Do not promise an exact response time unless you can consistently meet it.
  5. Ownership: a named team member reviews the enquiry, asks a few clarifying questions and schedules a visit if needed.
  6. Follow-through: the estimate has an owner, a real deadline and a respectful next check-in. Promotional messages require separate consideration.

Either platform may cover pieces of this flow. The decisive question is which system keeps those pieces connected without introducing expensive complexity. Our quote-request form guide provides a reusable field checklist.

Do not compare the headline monthly price alone

The published Systeme.io pricing page currently lists a free plan with capped funnels and automations, plus paid tiers. SITE123 publishes a free builder and premium tiers, but commercial terms are binding at checkout and may vary by promotion, package, term or geography. Rather than reproduce a static price that will go stale, verify each checkout in your own country and currency.

Free two-minute website-or-funnel decision worksheet

Use this original checklist in a meeting with your team. Write down the answers; it is a planning exercise, not a recommendation generated from private customer information.

Start with the buyer's journey

  1. What do visitors search for? A contractor, a project gallery, a specific service, or a single offer?
  2. Which pages must exist? Services, service areas, actual examples, About, contact and privacy information?
  3. What happens after a form submission? Who receives it, and where is the next action tracked?
  4. Will you send follow-up emails? Transactional responses only, or consent-based marketing campaigns?
  5. What can you afford to maintain? One system, or a website plus separate marketing funnel?
A simple decision ruleIf you cannot answer questions 1–3 yet, work on the service website and response process first. If those are already solved and you're planning campaigns or segmented email follow-up, test the funnel workflow. If you only need to stop losing estimates, fix the lead handoff before buying anything.
